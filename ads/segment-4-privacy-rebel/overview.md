## Target Audience

**Who they are:**
Privacy-conscious individuals who are deeply skeptical of surveillance capitalism.
Tech workers, journalists, digital rights advocates, general skeptics of Big Tech.
They use Signal instead of WhatsApp, have a password manager, probably use a VPN,
might use Linux. They follow EFF, The Markup, privacy subreddits.
They want to listen to audiobooks but refuse to give Audible/Amazon/Spotify their data.

**Demographics:** 25–45, tech-adjacent, above-average income, principled consumer.

**State of mind when they see this ad:** Watching YouTube (maybe with uBlock Origin on
desktop, or YouTube Premium to avoid ads — so if they're seeing this, they're on mobile).
The first 5 seconds must feel like the ad is speaking *directly to them*, not advertising.

---

## Why This Segment Converts

1. **Identity-driven purchase** — for this segment, using LoudReader is a statement.
   They're not just buying an app; they're rejecting surveillance capitalism.
2. **No alternatives exist** — Audible is Amazon. Spotify collects data. Libby requires
   a library card and collects some data. There is genuinely no fully offline,
   zero-account competitor.
3. **Premium conversion is nearly certain** — they will pay for the "right" company.
   They already pay for Signal, ProtonMail, Mullvad. This is their stack.
4. **Advocacy value is enormous** — one post on Hacker News, r/privacy, or Mastodon
   from this segment can generate thousands of installs. High CAC ROI.
5. **The silence at the end of the ad** — intentional, matching their values.
   No hype, no noise. That speaks to them.
