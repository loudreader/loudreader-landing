## Purpose

Make the 70,000-book library feel real, beautiful, and personally relevant.
For this segment, the Gutenberg catalog isn't just a feature — it's a lifetime
of reading made instantly available. The author name call-outs personalize it:
"your Dostoyevsky, your Austen, your Kafka — they're all already in here."

---

## Visual Description

**Primary visual:** Screen recording of LoudReader's Project Gutenberg library browser.

The camera slowly pans across the grid of classic book covers — they should be beautiful,
recognizable, iconic. The grid moves slowly from left to right, or scrolls gently downward.

Key covers to feature (where possible in the UI):
- Fyodor Dostoyevsky — Crime and Punishment, The Brothers Karamazov
- Jane Austen — Pride and Prejudice, Sense and Sensibility
- Jules Verne — Twenty Thousand Leagues, Around the World in 80 Days
- Charles Dickens — Great Expectations, A Tale of Two Cities
- Franz Kafka — The Metamorphosis, The Trial
- Leo Tolstoy — War and Peace, Anna Karenina
- Victor Hugo — Les Misérables
- Homer — The Odyssey

The UI itself should be the warm, clean LoudReader design — the grid of covers should
look curated and beautiful, not like a database dump.

**Author name overlays:**
As each author is mentioned in the V/O, their name appears briefly in elegant typography
in the center of the screen — and then fades:

```
Dostoyevsky.    → fades in/out (2s)
Austen.         → fades in/out (2s)
Verne.          → fades in/out (2s)
Kafka.          → fades in/out (2s)
```

**Typography for overlays:** Serif font (or Space Grotesk Light italic).
White text, slight drop shadow for legibility on book covers. Not bold — elegant.
