## Target Audience

**Who they are:**
People who save articles to read later — Pocket, Instapaper, Safari Reading List,
emailed newsletters — and never actually read them. They care about staying
informed but their "read later" pile is a graveyard of good intentions.
They want to consume more quality news and long-form journalism but can't
find time to sit down and read.

**Demographics:** 25–40, news-engaged, probably saves NYT/The Atlantic/Substack
articles. Commutes daily. Slightly overwhelmed by information volume.

---

## How This Works (Important Context)

LoudReader imports EPUB and PDF files. News content reaches LoudReader via:

1. **PDF export** — any article or newsletter can be "Print to PDF" from Safari/Chrome
   and imported directly into LoudReader
2. **Pocket / Instapaper EPUB export** — both services can export saved articles
   as EPUB files readable by LoudReader
3. **Newsletter EPUB** — many newsletters (Substack, etc.) offer EPUB or PDF export
4. **RSS-to-EPUB services** — several tools convert RSS feeds to EPUB for offline reading

**The workflow is real, just requires a small setup step.** The ad should acknowledge
this naturally — frame it as "the workflow" rather than hiding it. The audience is
tech-comfortable enough to set this up once and use it forever.

## Why This Segment Converts

1. **Massive, universal pain point** — the "read later" pile is a modern epidemic.
   Everyone with a smartphone has one. The recognition hook is instant.
2. **Immediate utility** — within minutes of downloading, they can save an article
   as PDF and have it read to them. No waiting.
3. **Offline = commute-ready** — the main reading-dead-zone (subway) is where
   people most want to catch up on news.
4. **Complements existing habits** — they don't have to stop subscribing to the NYT.
   They just start actually consuming it.
5. **The workflow is a feature** — for this tech-forward audience, knowing *how*
   it works (PDF/EPUB pipeline) is satisfying, not off-putting.
